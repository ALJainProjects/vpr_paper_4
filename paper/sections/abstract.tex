\begin{abstract}
Place recognition by image retrieval is an established route to
relocalization in colonoscopy, and an off-the-shelf retrieval model has
already been evaluated on colonoscopy ground truth. How much of the
remaining error a diverse zoo of frozen representations could recover has,
as far as we found, not been measured. We specify a study that transfers the
modern visual place recognition (VPR) zoo, frozen and without medical
fine-tuning, to colonoscopy and splits its error with a zoo-relative oracle
ceiling into a selection-recoverable part (some zoo member retrieves the
right place) and a zoo-unsolved part (no member does, for this zoo, ground
truth and exclusion window). The zoo spans CNN- and DINOv2-based VPR models,
AnyLoc and raw DINOv2, beside a frozen endoscopy-pretrained arm. Ground truth
is endoscopist-verified same-place intervals on real sequences (Colon10k) and
view overlap from depth and pose on silicone phantoms (C3VD, C3VDv2), whose
results make no claim about patients. Quantities are reported per ground
truth, exclusion window and model arm, beside a random-ranking chance
ceiling, and unsolved real queries are audited for ground-truth misses before
any reading as view ambiguity. Controlled phantom
contrasts support a feature-reliance diagnosis. Two-sided hypotheses and a
go/no-go gate are specified here; their thresholds are to be fixed and
publicly registered before any data are acquired, and none is set yet.
