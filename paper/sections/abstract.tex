\begin{abstract}
Place recognition by image retrieval is an established route to
relocalization in colonoscopy, and an off-the-shelf retrieval model has
already been evaluated on colonoscopy ground truth. What has not been
measured is how much of the remaining error \emph{any} available
representation can recover. We pre-register a study that transfers the
modern visual place recognition (VPR) zoo, frozen and without medical
fine-tuning, to colonoscopy and splits its error with a zoo-relative oracle
ceiling into a recoverable part (some zoo member retrieves the right place)
and an irreducible part (none does). The zoo spans CNN- and DINOv2-based VPR
models, AnyLoc and raw DINOv2, beside a frozen endoscopy-pretrained arm.
Ground truth is endoscopist-verified same-place intervals on real sequences
(Colon10k) and view overlap from depth and pose on silicone phantoms (C3VD,
C3VDv2); all phantom results are in-silico. Every quantity is reported per
ground truth, temporal-exclusion window and feature family, against the
ceiling that random rankings reach by chance. Controlled phantom contrasts
support a feature-reliance diagnosis, and a frozen probe tests
anatomical-section recognition across patients and centres. A two-sided
primary hypothesis and a go/no-go gate are fixed before any data are seen.
