\begin{abstract}
Camera-carrying miniature in-body robots, such as robotic and magnetically
actuated capsule endoscopes and robotic flexible endoscopes, are localized
mainly by external sensing or kinematics; their cameras serve
odometry, organ classification and steering. We test whether frozen,
street-trained visual place recognition (VPR) models could supply a missing
component, relocalization from the robot's own images, on conventional
colonoscopy video rather than robot data. The thresholds, model zoo,
comparator and case split were fixed in a signed internal pre-registration
before any model was run, and the go/no-go gate was scored once on held-out
cases. On \res{gate-ncases} test cases of Colon10k (\res{gate-nq} answerable
queries, endoscopist-verified same-place intervals, a
\res{gate-window}-frame exclusion window), all \res{gate-nbeat} frozen models
beat chance and \res{gate-nnontriv} beat a raw-pixel thumbnail at the lower
bound of a case-level bootstrap interval, so the gate returned
\texttt{\res{gate-outcome}}. The best model reached Recall@1
\res{pm-boqr50-r1} (\res{gate-cilevel} interval \res{pm-boqr50-lo} to
\res{pm-boqr50-hi}), against \res{bl-raw-r1} for the thumbnail. At least
one zoo member retrieves the right place for \res{or-ceil} of queries, and
\res{or-irr} are solved by none, for this zoo, ground truth and window. An
informational validation readout had matched the rule's owner-review
pattern; the unchanged rule gave \texttt{\res{gate-outcome}} on test, with
wide intervals. This is a perception-component measurement: no robot,
capsule or closed loop was involved, and no clinical claim is made.

\end{abstract}
