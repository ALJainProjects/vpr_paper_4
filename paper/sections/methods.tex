\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Frozen models}
\label{sec:zoo}
The \emph{outdoor arm} $\Zout$ holds CNN-based models
(NetVLAD~\cite{arandjelovic2016netvlad}, CosPlace~\cite{berton2022rethinking},
EigenPlaces~\cite{berton2023eigenplaces}, MixVPR~\cite{alibey2023mixvpr},
BoQ~\cite{alibey2024boq} on ResNet-50) and DINOv2-based ones
(SALAD~\cite{izquierdo2024optimal}, MegaLoc~\cite{berton2025megaloc}, BoQ
on DINOv2). The \emph{generic arm} $\Zgen$ holds raw
DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} (CLS token; GeM-pooled patch tokens) at the
backbone size and input resolution of each model it is contrasted with, and
AnyLoc-VLAD~\cite{keetha2024anyloc} with a vocabulary released for another
domain, which is strictly zero-shot. AnyLoc-VLAD with a vocabulary built from
the target database is a separate \emph{target-adapted} condition, outside
every zero-shot claim; AnyLoc-GeM joins if a wrapper is written. The frozen
\emph{medical arm} $\Zmed$ holds EndoViT~\cite{batic2024endovit} (surgical
pretraining), Endo-FM~\cite{wang2023foundation} (video) and the colonoscopy
encoders of~\cite{hirsch2023selfsupervised}; the landmark-trained anchor
of~\cite{morlana2021selfsupervised} joins $\Zout$ if its weights prove
obtainable. $\Zall$ is the union of the arms. A \emph{backbone family} is
the set of CNN-based, of DINOv2-based (with $\Zgen$) or of medical members:
the CNN and DINOv2 families share no backbone, whereas $\Zout$ and $\Zgen$
do. \pending{per model: checkpoint, input size, dimension; Endo-FM frame
recipe} No parameter is updated:
reference-set fine-tuning, adapters and re-ranking are excluded, and some
zoo-unsolved error may be solved by them. Descriptors are
L2-normalized and ranked by exhaustive cosine similarity.

\subsection{Datasets and ground truth}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Colon10k (real).} Twenty short sequences (cases) from one centre,
undistorted to low-resolution pinhole frames~\cite{ma2021colon10k}. Per case,
a matchings file lists for each annotated query the intervals of frames an
endoscopist verified as the same place; its interval syntax, frame-id
convention and end inclusivity are undocumented and are read off the first
file before any model is run. Positives are the database frames inside the
query's intervals; frames within $W$ frame ids of the query leave database
and positives, as near-duplicates inflate
recall~\cite{morlana2021selfsupervised}; queries left without a positive are
dropped and counted. The database is the query's case or all cases
(Table~\ref{tab:prereg}). Every frame outside the intervals is a negative,
although whether the annotation is exhaustive is unknown (audit,
Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}). Totals are counted at acquisition:
\pending{Colon10k frame, query, positive counts}. We could not
read Colon10k's own protocol: this is our reading of its release, and we
compare with none of its numbers.

\paragraph{C3VD and C3VDv2 (phantom).} Colonoscopy video of silicone
phantoms with per-frame pose and depth~\cite{bobrow2023colonoscopy}; C3VDv2
has two sculpted geometries with four textures each and, for every
debris-laden video, a clean twin on the identical
trajectory~\cite{golhar2026c3vdv2}. E2 pairs recordings of one phantom and
texture made on different trajectories (queries from one, database from the
other); twins enter only E4. Video pairs and resampling clusters (phantom
segments, not recordings, which share databases) are fixed from the
release's structure before any model is run. That different recordings'
poses share one phantom frame, via the 2D--3D registration, is an
\emph{unverified precondition} of every cross-recording comparison. The
primary ground truth, \emph{view overlap}, is not yet implemented: the
fraction of a query's valid pixels (in the field of view, with depth in the
released valid range) whose back-projected points, mapped by both poses and
projected through the released fisheye calibration, land in the database
view with depth agreeing within $\varepsilon_d$; it is directional (query to
database) and positive at $o^\star$ or above. The secondary one, \emph{pose
and angle} (implemented), needs camera centres within $\tau$ and optical
axes within $\theta$. Pose radius alone is never used: nearby cameras in a
tube may share no view~\cite{larsen2026longterm}.

\paragraph{Preprocessing.} If enabled (Table~\ref{tab:prereg}), pixels
outside the field of view (largest bright connected component: max-channel
threshold, opening, hole filling) or specular (bright and near-achromatic:
HSV thresholds, dilated) are set to a constant fill colour before a
mask-aware crop and resize to each model's input; nothing is inpainted or
dropped. Defaults are untuned; the unmodified street pipeline is an ablation.

\subsection{Zoo ceiling and error decomposition}
\label{sec:ceiling}
Let $c_m(q)\in\{0,1\}$ mark that model $m$'s top-$k$ list for query $q$
contains a positive under a given ground truth and window, and $R_m$ its
mean (Recall@$k$). For a zoo $Z$, let $o_Z(q)=\max_{m\in Z}c_m(q)$; the
\emph{zoo ceiling} (after~\cite{waheed2021improving}) is
$C(Z)=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_q o_Z(q)$ and the \emph{zoo-unsolved} fraction is
$I(Z)=1-C(Z)$. Each member's error splits exactly as
$1-R_m=\rho_m(Z)+I(Z)$, with
$\rho_m(Z)=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_q \bigl(1-c_m(q)\bigr)\,o_Z(q)$: queries $m$
misses but another member solves. $I(Z)$ is an oracle miss rate, not a
Bayes error, and nothing in this paper estimates one.

\emph{What $C(Z)$ bounds.} On this query set, ground truth, window and $k$,
$C(Z)$ (i)~upper-bounds the Recall@$k$ of any per-query selector that
returns one member's top-$k$ list (routing, cascades, plurality voting over
top-1 answers); (ii)~lower-bounds the fraction of recognizable queries only
up to chance hits, which inflate it, and ground-truth artefacts, which can
move it either way; (iii)~bounds no model outside $Z$; and (iv)~does not
bound score fusion, ranked-list voting, re-ranking or adaptation, which can
return an item no member ranks first. So $\rho_m$ is the
\emph{selection-recoverable} part of $m$'s error, the only sense of
``recoverable'' used here, and $C(Z)-\max_m R_m$ is the headroom for
selection.

\emph{Chance.} A random ranking puts a positive among the top $k$ with
probability $p_q=1-\binom{N_q-P_q}{k}\big/\binom{N_q}{k}$, for $P_q$
positives among $N_q$ candidates. The chance ceiling of $n$ independent
random rankings is $\Crand(n)=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_q\bigl[1-(1-p_q)^{n}\bigr]$,
and adding $n'$ of them to $Z$ is expected to lower $I$ by
$\Delta I_{\mathrm{rand}}(Z,n')=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_q\bigl(1-o_Z(q)\bigr)
\bigl[1-(1-p_q)^{n'}\bigr]$: a union over many models collects chance hits,
and Colon10k's positive intervals can be wide. The zoo-size curve $\bar
C(n)$ is the mean of $C(S)$ over subsets $S\subseteq Z$ of size $n$; $m$'s
leave-one-out contribution is $C(Z)-C(Z\setminus\{m\})$.

\subsection{Baselines and statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
Two training-free baselines anchor ``non-trivial'': the exact random-ranking
rate $p_q$ (mean $\Rrand$; random rankings never enter a zoo) and \emph{one}
handcrafted descriptor fixed before data, a raw-pixel thumbnail compared by
normalized cross-correlation or a Hellinger-normalized joint RGB histogram.
Intervals are cluster percentile bootstraps over whole clusters (Colon10k
cases; phantom segments), each replicate the pooled mean over their queries.
$\LB$ and $\UB$ are the ends of a central interval of level
$1-\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}$, i.e.\ one-sided bounds at level
$1-\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}/2$, with $\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}=2\alpha/|\Zgate|$ for
the gate (Bonferroni over the gate zoo) and $2\alpha$ otherwise. Paired
contrasts get two-sided $p$-values (twice the smaller share of replicates
beyond zero, as $(b+1)/(B+1)$), Holm-corrected within H1a (one test per
member), H1b (per DINOv2-based VPR model and raw variant) and H4b (per
manipulation). With twenty or fewer clusters, percentile intervals may
under-cover. There is no validation split, and nothing is tuned on
evaluation data.
