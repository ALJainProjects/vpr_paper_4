\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under an internal pre-registration fixed before any model was run, a zoo of
frozen VPR models, most of them street-trained, found the same place in real
colonoscopy far above chance, and \res{gate-nnontriv} of \res{gate-nzoo} did so detectably
better than raw pixels: the one-shot gate returned
\texttt{\res{gate-outcome}}. Some member solves \res{or-ceil} of the queries,
and \res{or-rec-share} of the best member's errors are solved by another
member, so per-query selection has headroom, while \res{or-irr} remain
zoo-unsolved for this zoo, ground truth and window. For camera-carrying
in-body robots, this makes frozen appearance-based relocalization a
candidate complement to external sensing that is worth testing on robot
video; it does not show that it works on a robot. No clinical claim is made.
