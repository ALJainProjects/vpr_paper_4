\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Miniature in-body robots that carry a camera, such as robotic and
magnetically actuated capsule endoscopes and robotic flexible endoscopes,
need their position, for instance to retarget a lesion at
follow-up~\cite{bianchi2019localization}. For capsules it comes mainly from
external magnetic or radio-frequency
sensing~\cite{bianchi2019localization,cao2024robotic}; in a magnetic flexible
endoscope, magnetic sensing localizes and vision only centres the
lumen~\cite{martin2020enabling}. Capsule cameras serve visual odometry and
segment classification~\cite{herp2021feature}; a recent review
of capsule video localization lists only topographical segmentation and
motion estimation~\cite{cao2024robotic}, and capsule-robot SLAM has omitted
global loop closure because ``the inner organ volumes are quite
small''~\cite{turan2017nonrigid}. Revisiting a chosen point has been shown on
a capsule robot with magnetic localization~\cite{xu2022autonomous}, which is
millimetre-accurate in controlled settings~\cite{cao2024robotic}; the authors
of a magnetic endoscope call its assumption of an undistorted field ``a
challenging restriction in clinical environments''~\cite{martin2020enabling}. Recognizing a previously seen place
from the robot's own images would therefore be a complement to external
sensing, not a replacement. The one robot platform we found running place
recognition used handcrafted bag-of-words inside ORB-SLAM2 and was assessed
only visually~\cite{prendergast2018autonomous}. We do not address
nanorobots, which carry no camera and are localized by external medical
imaging~\cite{aziz2020medical}.

In colonoscopy, relocalization by image retrieval is
established~\cite{ma2021colon10k,morlana2024colonmapper}, and an
off-the-shelf, landmark-trained retrieval network has already been evaluated
on real colonoscopy~\cite{morlana2021selfsupervised}. Street-trained visual
place recognition (VPR) has advanced quickly since, and frozen VPR models
transfer to some texture-poor domains~\cite{keetha2024anyloc,larsen2026longterm}.
To our knowledge (search to September 2026), no prior work evaluates
general-purpose, street-trained VPR models for localizing camera-carrying
in-body robots. We ask two questions of a zoo of such models, frozen and
without medical fine-tuning: do they retrieve the same place in the colon
better than raw pixels, and how much of their error could per-query model
selection recover? Our data are conventional colonoscope video with
endoscopist-verified same-place ground truth~\cite{ma2021colon10k}, not robot
video: we measure a perception component, not a robot.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{C1}] A zero-shot test of \res{gate-nzoo} frozen models under an
  internal pre-registration, with a go/no-go gate scored once on held-out
  cases, reported with the protocol's full history, including the decisions
  taken after an informational validation readout
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item[\textbf{C2}] A zoo ceiling, the any-correct oracle over a model zoo
  (after~\cite{waheed2021improving}), measured for the first time on
  endoscopic place-recognition ground truth as far as we found. It splits
  the error into a selection-recoverable and a zoo-unsolved part, beside a
  chance ceiling.
\item[\textbf{C3}] A descriptive, explicitly cross-protocol comparison with
  the same models' street recall.
\end{itemize}
