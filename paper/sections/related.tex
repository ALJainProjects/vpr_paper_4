\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Place recognition in the body.}
Retrieval-based place recognition in the gut includes retargeting across
examinations~\cite{ye2017image}, the Colon10k benchmark~\cite{ma2021colon10k},
retrieval trained on structure-from-motion
clusters~\cite{morlana2021selfsupervised}, and topological mapping on
fine-tuned descriptors~\cite{morlana2024colonmapper,morlana2024topological}
over EndoMapper~\cite{azagra2023endomapper}. Section classification is an
established supervised task~\cite{biffi2025temporal}; E3 only probes it.
Zero-shot use is not new either: beyond the off-the-shelf baseline
of~\cite{morlana2021selfsupervised}, NetVLAD retrieves candidate views in
ureteroscopy~\cite{li2026automated}, a NetVLAD-distilled network does so in
laparoscopy~\cite{wei2023stereo}, off-the-shelf local matchers (LoFTR with
outdoor weights, LightGlue) run inside ColonMapper and
ColonSLAM~\cite{morlana2024colonmapper,morlana2024topological},
and a training-free foundation-model method re-identifies regions after scope
reinsertion~\cite{soberanismukul2026global} (its model is not named in the
record we could access). None measures per-model retrieval quality or its
ceiling; frozen general and endoscopy foundation models have been compared
only for object-level retrieval~\cite{parolari2026contrastive}.

\paragraph{Zero-shot VPR and natural-to-medical transfer.}
Moving a frozen VPR zoo to a texture-poor domain is an established
genre~\cite{keetha2024anyloc,larsen2026longterm,gorry2025imagebased}, none
of it medical; we borrow its protocol and add the domain and the
ceiling. The value of natural-image pretraining for medical imaging is
contested~\cite{raghu2019transfusion}, but frozen DINOv2 is strong: it beats
ImageNet features on medical classification~\cite{huix2024natural} and
matches an endoscopy-pretrained DINOv2 on GI landmarks~\cite{dermyer2025endodino}. Endoscopy foundation
models~\cite{wang2023foundation,batic2024endovit,hirsch2023selfsupervised,jong2026gastronet5m}
are usually evaluated after fine-tuning and, as far as we found, never on
frozen same-place retrieval.

\paragraph{Ceilings, ambiguity and feature reliance.}
The oracle ceiling---the fraction of queries that at least one method
localizes---was introduced for VPR, for pairs of techniques, as an estimate
of the maximum achievable performance~\cite{waheed2021improving}; we apply it
to a zoo, with the error split of~\cite{vasconcelos2004minimum}. TOPO-Bench
already measures dataset ambiguity across indoor and outdoor environments
and argues that it is largely method-independent~\cite{wang2025topobench}, so
C4 concerns only the oracle-ceiling construct. Repetition breaks retrieval~\cite{torii2015visual}, distance-threshold
ground truth overestimates recall~\cite{larsen2026longterm,wei2025breaking},
and set-up choices make VPR results hard to compare across
papers~\cite{schubert2021makes}. The texture-bias account of CNNs is
contested: under controlled cue suppression
they rely mostly on local shape, and medical-imaging models emphasize
colour~\cite{burgert2025imagenettrained}. VPR descriptors can retrieve by
condition as well as by place~\cite{kim2026visual}, and colour helps only
where chromatic cues are persistent and discriminative~\cite{ismagilov2026channel}.
